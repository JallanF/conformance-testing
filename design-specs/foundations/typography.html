Typography

Type does the talking. A display serif that appears rarely and lands hard, mono eyebrows that give panels a machined precision, and a quiet sans carrying everything else.

The three faces

--font-sans Inter. Everything that is not display, and not a number in a column.
--font-mono Commit Mono. Eyebrows, IDs, money, timestamps.
--font-serif Instrument Serif. The display voice — and nothing else.

Inter is deliberate and mandated. It is not a placeholder to be upgraded to something more distinctive.

Numbers in columns — mono, tabular, right-aligned

Tabular figures make the decimal points line up — that is the whole point, and it is why a proportional face is wrong here. Note the last row: a minus sign for negatives, never accounting parentheses.

Money, IDs, timestamps

Money: one currency at a time — the shipment's own, never dual CAD/USD. Two decimals, locale thousands separators, minus sign for negatives. Never abbreviate a money value (chart axes may).

Timestamps: relative by default ("2 h ago"). Absolute timestamps with an explicit timezone in detail metadata and on legal surfaces — invoices, customs documents. A cross-border document never shows a bare time.

No italics in UI text. Underline is reserved for links.
